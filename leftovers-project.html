A map tracing the story of Villa Epecuén, a village in Argentina. In 1985, the neighboring saltwater lake flooded the village, and within a few days the entire village lay under 10 meters of saltwater.
It stayed that way for 20 years. In 2009, following a severe drought, the lake began to recede, gradually revealing a ghost town.

The map tells the town’s story across
a 40-year timeline, using the village as a case study of the endless battle between humans and nature. Working only with data and hard facts, I had to find a way to tell a powerful story about leftovers, and to bring forward what most people would rather forget, or not see at all.

The entire project is based on reliable data and satellite imagery, which served as my anchor throughout

Process

The research had two layers. The first came from above, satellite images from Google Earth, across the years the water moved. I placed them one over the other, isolated the water and the buildings, and stacked them in transparent turquoise layers. The longer an area was underwater, the deeper its color.

The second layer came from inside the village. I looked for places that mattered to the people who lived there before it was lost, and marked them on the map. Their photos became illustrations in the map’s language, so the small stories of the people stay inside the big picture seen from space.

Looking Back

From this project, I learned to edit a story. I wanted to tell every layer of it, and the 70×50 cm map ended up holding more detail than it needed. Looking back, cutting details early would have told the same story more clearly.
